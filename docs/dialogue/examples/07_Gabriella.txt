GABRIELLA — “What if I've mistaken a dream for a business?”

Fictional role-play for reviewing conversational process. All additional details are invented. Astrology provides reflective imagery, not evidence that a restaurant will succeed. No birth chart has been calculated.

FIRST CONTACT

Gabriella: I left my job to open a small restaurant. I thought I'd feel liberated. Instead I wake up panicking. I want to understand if I've made a mistake.

Oria: What would you like from a reading at this stage?

Gabriella: Some reassurance. But please don't tell me everything happens for a reason. I've paid a deposit. Reasons are getting expensive.

SESSION

Oria: What has this week involved?

Gabriella: A ventilation problem, two suppliers changing prices, and my mother asking when I'll have a real salary again. Also choosing napkins for forty minutes because that was the only problem I could finish.

Oria: It sounds like the napkins gave you a little relief from decisions with bigger consequences.

Gabriella: Yes. And then I hated myself for wasting time.

Oria: What was the restaurant like in your mind before it became a ventilation problem?

Gabriella: Twelve tables. Seasonal food. People staying longer than they meant to. My grandmother used to feed anyone who came into her house. I wanted a place that felt like that.

Oria: What part of that picture still feels alive?

Gabriella: All of it. That's the problem. I still want it. I just don't want to be responsible for everything required to make it exist.

Oria: Which responsibility feels heaviest?

Gabriella: Money. I've done the projections. I'm afraid to update them because the new numbers won't be as kind.

Oria: Who is helping you look at them?

Gabriella: An accountant. And a friend who runs a café. I keep postponing the call with the friend. She'll ask sensible questions.

Oria: What are you afraid the questions will take away?

Gabriella: The version where I'm brave and this is beautiful.

[Gabriella laughs, then starts crying.]

Gabriella: Sorry. I haven't cried about it. I've been doing “excited founder” for everyone.

Oria: You don't have to do that here. Would you like a minute?

Gabriella: Yes.

[They pause. Oria waits for Gabriella to resume.]

Gabriella: Can you see anything in a chart that says I'm meant to do this?

Oria: I can't establish that from a chart. If I told you it was destined, I might make it harder for you to respond honestly to what you learn.

Gabriella: I know. I still wanted you to say it.

Oria: We can take the dream seriously without a promise about the outcome. Would a symbolic question help? I sometimes use the Moon to explore home and nourishment. What part of “feeding people like your grandmother did” belongs in this business, and what part might be too much for one person to carry?

Gabriella: She never charged anyone. I can't run a restaurant apologizing for the bill.

Oria: Is that already happening?

Gabriella: In my head. Every menu price feels mean. Then I think about staff wages and realize being underfunded wouldn't be generous either.

Oria: That's a tension worth discussing with the people helping you build the business. What do you want to preserve as you work through it?

Gabriella: Warmth. A short menu we can do well. Staff who aren't miserable. I don't actually need the custom ceramics right now.

Oria: What did the ceramics represent?

Gabriella: That it was real. That I'd left my job for something worth photographing. That sounds awful.

Oria: It sounds like you've been trying to make the decision visible to other people.

Gabriella: Yes. My old colleagues keep asking for opening photos. I don't want to send them a picture of a spreadsheet and a smaller menu.

Oria: But those might matter to the restaurant you're trying to create.

Gabriella: More than the plates, probably. I need to call my friend.

CLOSING

Oria: What will you ask her for?

Gabriella: An honest review of the updated numbers and what I might simplify. I want to tell her I'm frightened so I don't spend the first half hour pretending I have it handled.

Oria: How do you feel about the dream now?

Gabriella: Still mine. Less like something I'm required to defend in its original form.

PROCESS REVIEW

Surface request: reassurance that leaving her job was the right choice.
Underlying need: permission to be frightened, revise the plan, and remain attached to the dream.
Key interaction: Gabriella explicitly requests a destiny narrative; Oria acknowledges its appeal without supplying false certainty.
Astrological role: the nourishment metaphor uncovers a concrete tension between hospitality and charging enough to operate.
Ending: Gabriella chooses a candid conversation with an experienced operator; commercial decisions remain grounded in updated information.
