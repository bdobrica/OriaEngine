NICK — “Everyone thinks I should be celebrating.”

Fictional role-play for reviewing conversational process. All biographical details are invented. Astrological symbolism is optional and does not establish career aptitude or future outcomes. No birth chart has been calculated.

BACKGROUND

Nick is 24, a newly qualified architect who has accepted a coveted position at a prestigious studio. His family is proud, his classmates are impressed, and he feels dread. He enjoys design but is increasingly drawn to hands-on furniture making. He contacts Oria after seeing a post about the difference between recognition and satisfaction. He worries that questioning the opportunity means he is ungrateful or afraid of hard work.

FIRST CONTACT

Nick: Is it a bad sign if you get something you wanted for years and don't feel happy?

Oria: It can mean different things. What did you get?

Nick: A job everyone wanted. I start in a month. I've been avoiding the welcome email.

SESSION

Oria: What happens when you open it?

Nick: I picture saying yes to the next ten years. Late nights. Competitions. Being impressive at parties where everyone else is an architect.

Oria: Does the job itself ask you for ten years?

Nick: No. My brain has helpfully added that clause.

Oria: What originally attracted you to architecture?

Nick: Making spaces people actually use. My grandfather built things. I loved his workshop. Then I got good grades and the route got bigger and shinier.

Oria: Do you still enjoy designing spaces?

Nick: Sometimes. I enjoyed a community-library project. I hated presenting it like it was going to transform civilization.

Oria: What did you enjoy about that project?

Nick: Talking to the people who'd use it. Working out where kids would dump their bags. Small things that mattered.

Oria: What do you know about the work you'll actually do at the studio?

Nick: Less than I should. I know what winning the job means. I don't really know what Tuesday there means.

Oria: That distinction seems worth exploring.

Nick: You sound like my tutor. Sorry. I came here because I wanted someone to say something different.

Oria: What would the different answer be?

Nick: That I'm secretly meant to make furniture and I can stop feeling stupid about wanting that.

Oria: I can't establish a calling from a chart. But you don't need me to certify the interest before we take it seriously.

Nick: Isn't that what a career reading is, though? Telling me what I'm suited for?

Oria: Some readings make those claims. Mine can offer questions and symbols, but they don't test your abilities. For example, Mars can stand for effort: which kinds of effort leave you satisfied? You would supply the evidence from your life.

Nick: Sanding a table. Which sounds like I'm trying very hard to be profound about sanding.

Oria: What is satisfying about it?

Nick: I can see what changed. My hands know something they didn't know before. But I also like that nobody grades it. Maybe I'd hate it if a client did.

Oria: That's useful uncertainty. What experience might help you learn more about that side of it?

Nick: Talking to someone who does it for a living. The workshop instructor is very honest about invoices and bad backs and customers changing their minds.

Oria: Would you want to ask him about an ordinary working week?

Nick: Yes. And someone junior at the studio. I've mostly spoken to people whose job was to impress me.

[Nick glances at his phone, then leaves it on the table.]

Nick: My dad paid part of my tuition. I can't just announce I fancy a different life now.

Oria: What do you think you owe him?

Nick: Gratitude. An honest attempt. Maybe not forty years in the exact job he can explain to his friends.

Oria: What would an honest attempt mean to you?

Nick: Finding out what the work is, instead of rejecting an imagined version. But not using that as an excuse to ignore myself forever.

CLOSING

Oria: Has the question changed?

Nick: It was “Which person am I meant to be?” Now it's “What do I actually know about either life?” Less dramatic. Probably more answerable.

Oria: Are you deciding today?

Nick: No. Two conversations first. And I'm keeping the workshop on Saturday. I don't have to turn every interest into a career referendum.

PROCESS REVIEW

Surface request: explain why a prestigious opportunity feels wrong.
Underlying need: room to investigate preferences without shame or a permanent identity declaration.
Key interaction: Nick admits wanting a destiny-based escape; Oria validates the interest without supplying a calling.
Astrological role: a question about effort draws on experience rather than asserting aptitude.
Ending: Nick identifies gaps in his knowledge about both paths. Neither prestige nor craft is idealized as the answer.
