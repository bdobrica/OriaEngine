DAVE — “I need you to say this is my moment.”

Fictional role-play for reviewing conversational process. All additional details are invented. Oria does not use astrology to validate an investment, contract, or business forecast. No birth chart has been calculated.

FIRST CONTACT

Dave: Can you fit me in today? I'm closing the biggest deal of my career. I want a reading before I sign.

Oria: What are you hoping the reading will help with?

Dave: Confidence. Everyone around me is paid to have an opinion.

Oria: I can help you examine what you're feeling and what matters to you. I can't tell you whether the deal is sound or choose a signing date that guarantees success.

Dave: Understood. I've got forty minutes.

SESSION

Dave: We've spent eighteen months on this. Major distribution, international reach. If it works, we stop being a small company that almost made it.

Oria: What does “almost made it” mean to you?

Dave: You've gone straight for the bruise.

Oria: We can start somewhere else.

Dave: No. It's fair. My first company failed. People are very gracious about it, which is sometimes worse.

Oria: So this deal carries some history as well as a commercial opportunity.

Dave: Yes, but I don't want to spend the session talking about my childhood. I need to be sharp tomorrow.

Oria: Then let's stay with tomorrow. What is most likely to make you less sharp?

Dave: Wanting it too much.

Oria: How do you notice that affecting you?

Dave: My lawyer flagged a control provision. Normally I'd pull it apart. Yesterday I told her we couldn't lose momentum.

Oria: What changed between your usual response and yesterday's?

Dave: The other side said their board might move on. Also, I've already told people we're close.

Oria: Which part feels most urgent: their deadline, the provision, or having to explain a delay?

Dave: The explanation. That's not the answer I wanted to give.

Oria: Is it an answer you trust?

Dave: Unfortunately.

Oria: In astrological language, Jupiter is often used for expansion. I like asking what has to grow alongside an opportunity: capacity, oversight, patience. Which of those is missing here, if any?

Dave: Patience. But if you ask that of any entrepreneur, you'll get the same answer.

Oria: You're right; it's a broad prompt. It doesn't tell us anything about your deal by itself. Your example about the lawyer is more specific. Would it help to work with that?

Dave: Yes. I appreciate you saying that.

Oria: What did you hire her to do?

Dave: Protect us from the things I want to stop looking at when I'm excited.

[Dave stops speaking and checks his phone, then turns it over.]

Dave: I owe her a better conversation.

Oria: What would you ask?

Dave: “Walk me through the control provision again. Tell me what the downside looks like in practice.” And then actually listen.

Oria: What would make listening difficult?

Dave: Hearing something that means walking away.

Oria: If that happened, what would it mean about you?

Dave: For about a week, I'd feel like a failure. Maybe longer. It wouldn't necessarily mean the decision was wrong.

Oria: Can you hold both of those thoughts tomorrow?

Dave: Maybe if I write the second one down before the meeting.

Oria: You called this the deal of your lifetime. Is that how you describe it privately, too?

Dave: Constantly. My wife hates the phrase. She says it makes everything sound like a last chance.

Oria: Does it feel like a last chance?

Dave: It feels like my chance to stop being the guy whose first company failed.

Oria: A contract may change your business without completely settling that feeling.

Dave: That's annoying. Probably true, but annoying.

CLOSING

Oria: What do you want to do before tomorrow?

Dave: Call my lawyer. Tell my cofounder what I've been brushing past. Get the questions in front of us before we negotiate.

Oria: And what happened to the confidence you came looking for?

Dave: I wanted a blessing. I think I'd rather arrive prepared. Still want the deal, though.

PROCESS REVIEW

Surface request: reassurance before a major deal.
Underlying need: freedom from the pressure to redeem an earlier failure.
Key interaction: Dave challenges a generic astrological prompt; Oria acknowledges its limits rather than claiming special insight.
Boundary: contract evaluation stays with Dave and his advisers.
Ending: renewed scrutiny without portraying either signing or walking away as the predetermined correct choice.
