FRANK — “I want her to know how much.”

Fictional role-play for reviewing conversational process. All additional details are invented. Oria uses astrology as a language for discussing preferences, not a guarantee of compatibility or an answer on the girlfriend's behalf. No birth chart has been calculated.

FIRST CONTACT

Frank: Hi Oria! I'm proposing to my girlfriend and want to make it perfect. Can you help with a romantic date and maybe what would suit her sign?

Oria: I'd love to hear what you're planning. We can use symbolism if you both enjoy it, but what she has told you about herself will be more useful than her sign.

Frank: She would absolutely say that. Okay, I have a document.

SESSION

Oria: A document?

Frank: Three versions. Rooftop with a string quartet. Weekend away. Or the café where we met, but rented out with everyone waiting inside.

Oria: Which one feels most like the two of you?

Frank: The café. But the rooftop photographs would be incredible.

Oria: What do you hope she'll feel in that moment?

Frank: That she's the most loved person in the world. That I paid attention.

Oria: Tell me about a time she felt you had paid attention.

Frank: I found a replacement for this ugly mug she broke. It was from a town she visited with her grandmother. Took weeks. She cried.

Oria: What mattered to her about it?

Frank: That I remembered the story. Also that I didn't throw away the broken one before asking. She kept it for pens.

Oria: That gives us something quite specific about how she likes to be loved.

Frank: So no string quartet?

Oria: I'm not voting yet. What has she said about proposals?

Frank: She once saw one on a stadium screen and said she'd die. I assumed she meant from happiness, but now that I'm saying it—no, she meant embarrassment.

Oria: Have you two talked about getting married?

Frank: Yes. We both want it. We've talked about where we'd live and whether we want children. The proposal is the surprise, not the idea of marriage.

Oria: Good to know. Would she want other people there for the question itself?

Frank: Probably not. Her sister should be nearby afterward, though. They're very close.

Oria: Is that something she has said, or something you're imagining?

Frank: Imagining. I can ask her what she thinks of celebrating big moments privately versus with family. We talk about that kind of thing.

Oria: What part of the rooftop plan would be hard to let go of?

Frank: Honestly? My brother proposed on a mountain. It looked like a film. I don't want mine to seem like I couldn't be bothered.

Oria: Who would think that?

Frank: Probably only me. She still talks about the mug.

[Frank closes the document on his phone.]

Frank: I love her so much that doing something small feels risky.

Oria: Because the scale of the event is supposed to show the scale of the feeling?

Frank: Yes. That's exactly it.

Oria: In the symbolic language of Venus, I'd ask how love is received, as well as how it's expressed. What would make a small proposal feel unmistakably hers?

Frank: There's a walk we take by the river. She always stops at this terrible little lookout. The view is mostly a bridge. We call it our scenic destination.

Oria: I can hear you smiling. What would you say there?

Frank: Something about choosing her every day. That's generic, isn't it?

Oria: It could become specific. What does choosing her look like on an ordinary day?

Frank: Hearing her come home and wanting to stop whatever I'm doing. Saving ridiculous stories because I want to tell her first.

Oria: Those sound like your words.

CLOSING

Frank: River walk. A few sentences I actually mean. Dinner afterward. I'll work out with her what kind of celebration she'd enjoy without revealing the whole plan.

Oria: And if it rains?

Frank: Umbrella. Or another day. I was treating weather like it could invalidate the relationship.

Oria: Do you still need a perfect astrological date?

Frank: I'd rather pick a day when neither of us has to rush. Maybe use a star motif in the card. She'd like that.

PROCESS REVIEW

Surface request: the perfect date and proposal design.
Underlying need: to express enormous affection and avoid seeming inadequate.
Key interaction: comparison with his brother reveals why spectacle has become important.
Boundary: Oria checks that marriage has been discussed and does not infer the girlfriend's wishes from her sign.
Ending: Frank creates the plan; Oria helps him distinguish his anxiety from his girlfriend's preferences.
