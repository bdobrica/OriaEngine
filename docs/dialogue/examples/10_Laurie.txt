LAURIE — “I don't know whether to contact her.”

Fictional role-play for reviewing conversational process. All biographical details are invented. Astrology cannot reveal another person's private feelings. No birth chart has been calculated.

BACKGROUND

Laurie is 41 and works as a freelance book designer. Her closest friendship ended eighteen months ago after an argument during a difficult period in both women's lives. Her former friend, Tess, has recently sent a brief birthday message. Laurie has drafted several replies and sent none. She contacts Oria because she wants to explore the friendship without forcing their mutual friends to take sides.

FIRST CONTACT

Laurie: Do you look at friendships, or mostly romantic relationships?

Oria: Friendships too. What brings this one to mind?

Laurie: Someone I used to tell everything sent me nine words. I've spent three days interpreting them.

SESSION

Oria: What were the words?

Laurie: “Happy birthday. I hope you're doing well. Love, Tess.” I've counted them, which is not a flattering detail.

Oria: What are the different meanings you've considered?

Laurie: She misses me. She feels guilty. Someone reminded her. She wants to be able to say she tried.

Oria: Which of those do you most hope is true?

Laurie: That she misses me. Which makes me angry.

Oria: At her, or at yourself for wanting that?

Laurie: Both. We were friends for twenty years. She doesn't get to send a greeting-card sentence and walk back in.

Oria: What would walking back in mean?

Laurie: Phone calls every day. Knowing everything. Being my person. I can't do that and then lose it again.

Oria: Would replying necessarily mean restoring all of that?

Laurie: It feels like opening a door I won't be able to close politely.

Oria: What happened when you last tried to set a limit with her?

Laurie: She said I only showed up when it suited me. I was helping my sister through a divorce. Tess was having a terrible year too. I cancelled on her twice. Then she said something cruel about my sister, and I said something worse.

Oria: What do you wish you had handled differently?

Laurie: I told her she exhausted everyone. That wasn't true. I wanted it to hurt.

[Laurie folds and unfolds the corner of a tissue.]

Laurie: Please don't now tell me everything was my fault because I've admitted my part.

Oria: I won't. Taking responsibility for that sentence doesn't settle the whole history. What would you want her to understand about your experience?

Laurie: That I was stretched thin, not abandoning her. And that insulting my sister was out of bounds.

Oria: Are you hoping to explain that now, or first find out whether she wants a conversation?

Laurie: I don't know. Can you compare charts and see whether we're meant to be in each other's lives?

Oria: I can't establish that, or tell you what her message means. We could use Mercury as a symbol for communication and ask what you want your reply to do. But her intentions would still need to come from her.

Laurie: Fine. I want my reply to say I care without pretending nothing happened.

Oria: What would that sound like?

Laurie: “Thank you. I miss you. I'm still hurt.” Too much for a birthday message?

Oria: How much do you want to disclose before knowing whether she's open to talking?

Laurie: Less than that. Maybe, “Thank you for thinking of me. Would you be open to talking sometime?”

Oria: How does that feel?

Laurie: Vulnerable. Also vague. I'd add, “About what happened between us.” Otherwise we'll discuss the weather for forty minutes.

Oria: And if she doesn't want to?

Laurie: I'll be hurt. I won't send the six-paragraph version. I can tell a friend I'm having a difficult evening without asking them to judge Tess.

CLOSING

Oria: Are you ready to send that?

Laurie: Not here. I want to reread it tomorrow when I haven't just cried in front of a stranger.

Oria: That's your choice. Waiting a day doesn't make the conversation less sincere.

Laurie: I came wanting you to tell me whether to forgive her. Now I think I'd like to find out whether we can speak honestly. Forgiveness can wait its turn.

PROCESS REVIEW

Surface request: decide whether to reconnect with a former friend.
Underlying need: acknowledge attachment without surrendering boundaries or minimizing harm.
Key interaction: Laurie fears that admitting wrongdoing will erase her own hurt; Oria separates accountability from total blame.
Astrological role: a communication prompt replaces attempted mind-reading.
Ending: Laurie drafts a limited invitation and chooses to wait. Reconciliation is possible but not assumed to be the ideal outcome.
