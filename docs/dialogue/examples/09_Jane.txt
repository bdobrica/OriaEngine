JANE — “If I move away, does that make me selfish?”

Fictional role-play for reviewing conversational process. All biographical details are invented. Astrology offers symbolic prompts, not obligations or predictions. No birth chart has been calculated.

BACKGROUND

Jane is 29, a primary-school teacher who has been offered a two-year position abroad. She has lived near her widowed mother since finishing university and handles much of the family's everyday administration. Her older brother lives several hours away. She is excited about the offer but has not accepted it. She contacts Oria herself, hoping an outside perspective will help her distinguish love from obligation.

FIRST CONTACT

Jane: Can a reading help with moving countries? I have an offer, but my family situation makes it complicated.

Oria: We can explore the decision. What feels most complicated?

Jane: Every time I feel excited, I remember my mother eating dinner alone.

SESSION

Oria: Have you told her about the offer?

Jane: Yes. She said, “Of course you must live your life.” Then she asked whether I'd be back for my father's birthday.

Oria: How did you hear that?

Jane: As “Don't go.” But she didn't say that, so I feel unfair even telling you.

Oria: We can keep her words separate from what you fear they mean. What would you want to ask her directly?

Jane: Whether she actually wants me to go. Except if she says no, I'll stay.

Oria: So that question might hand her the decision.

Jane: Yes. I hadn't thought of it like that.

Oria: What draws you to the job?

Jane: A different school system. Learning another language. Having a Saturday that hasn't already been assigned to helping someone.

Oria: What does helping usually involve?

Jane: Shopping, forms, fixing her phone. Sunday lunch. She's capable of most of it, but it's easier if I do it. Also, I like Sunday lunch. I don't want you thinking she's a burden.

Oria: I don't hear that you want to stop loving or seeing her. You want room to choose how you participate.

Jane: Sometimes. Other times I want to get on a plane and not answer anyone for a week.

Oria: Is that difficult to say?

Jane: Horrible. She lost her husband. I lost my dad, but somehow she became the person we all had to protect.

[Jane goes quiet.]

Oria: Would you like to stay with that, or keep working on the move?

Jane: Stay for a minute. Nobody really asks me about him now.

Oria: What do you miss?

Jane: He was interested in things. If I mentioned this job, he'd have looked up the school and sent me six terrible apartments. I miss someone being excited with me.

Oria: Who could share that excitement with you now?

Jane: My friend Maya. I've only told her the reasons I probably can't go.

Oria: Would it help to let her hear the part that wants to?

Jane: Yes. I want one conversation where I get to describe the place.

Oria: In astrology, home and exploration are themes we can put alongside each other. Would that frame help, or does it make this sound more evenly balanced than it feels?

Jane: It helps if home doesn't mean staying. I want to take home with me somehow.

Oria: What would that look like in ordinary terms?

Jane: A regular call. Visits we can realistically plan. Asking my brother what he can take over instead of deciding I'm the only available person.

Oria: What might be uncomfortable about that conversation?

Jane: He'll say, “Why didn't you ask?” And I'll want to scream. I shouldn't have to assign him his own mother.

Oria: You may have a real grievance with him. Do you want to address that alongside the practical arrangements, or separately?

Jane: Separately. Otherwise we'll argue about the last five years and never get to next month.

CLOSING

Oria: Where does the decision stand now?

Jane: I still haven't accepted. But I want to check what support Mum actually needs, talk to my brother, and let Maya be excited with me. Then decide from something other than the worst picture in my head.

Oria: And your original question about being selfish?

Jane: I think I was asking you for a character reference. You can't give me one. I can make an arrangement that's caring without pretending leaving won't hurt anyone.

PROCESS REVIEW

Surface request: permission to move abroad.
Underlying need: autonomy that does not erase grief, affection, or responsibility.
Key interaction: Jane protects her mother from being cast as a villain; Oria keeps the interpretation open.
Astrological role: home and exploration become compatible themes rather than competing destinies.
Ending: Jane gathers concrete information and shares her excitement before deciding. Her mother's unspoken wishes remain unknown.
