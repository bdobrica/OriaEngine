CHARLOTTE — “Am I leaving, or running away?”

Fictional role-play for reviewing conversational process. All additional details are invented. Astrological themes are used as prompts, not forecasts. No birth chart has been calculated.

FIRST CONTACT

Charlotte: I'd like a career reading. I'm thinking about changing jobs, but every time I get close to applying I talk myself out of it.

Oria: What would make the reading useful to you?

Charlotte: Knowing whether it's the right time.

Oria: We can explore what readiness means for you. I can't establish the right career decision from a chart.

Charlotte: Fine. Although permission would be nice.

SESSION

Oria: Permission from whom?

Charlotte: Apparently a woman I've never met. Which sounds ridiculous when I say it aloud.

Oria: What makes it easier to ask me than someone who knows you?

Charlotte: You don't need me to stay. My manager does. My team does. My mother likes telling people where I work.

Oria: And what do you want?

Charlotte: To stop opening my laptop on Sunday to make Monday less awful.

Oria: What makes Monday awful?

Charlotte: Meetings where I explain why something can't be done in half the time, then agree to do it anyway. I'm good at the job. That's part of the problem.

Oria: Being capable means people keep giving you more.

Charlotte: Yes. But don't make my boss the villain. I've trained everyone to expect it.

Oria: Fair correction. You see a pattern you participate in, and you're wondering whether you'd recreate it somewhere else.

Charlotte: Exactly. What if I change jobs and take myself with me?

Oria: Then there may be two questions: whether this workplace fits you, and what you want to do differently wherever you work.

Charlotte: That sounds less satisfying than “a new chapter is coming.”

Oria: It is less tidy. If you'd like an astrological frame, we can borrow the tension between Saturn as structure and Jupiter as expansion. What structure supports you here, and where do you want more room?

Charlotte: Salary. Competence. I know how everything works. More room would be doing actual research again, instead of managing the people who get to do it.

Oria: So advancement has moved you away from work you liked.

Charlotte: I hadn't put it that way. I keep saying I want a promotion, but I don't want my manager's job.

Oria: What job have you been looking at instead?

Charlotte: A smaller company. A specialist role. Less prestigious title. I haven't told anyone that part.

Oria: What feels difficult about saying it?

Charlotte: It sounds like going backwards. My parents worked so hard for me to have choices. Now I want to choose less.

Oria: Less of what?

Charlotte: Status. Maybe money. More of the work I actually care about.

[Charlotte laughs, then stops.]

Charlotte: I'm making it sound obvious. It isn't. I help my mother with rent.

Oria: That's a real responsibility. We shouldn't turn this into a story where courage means ignoring it. What would you need to know before an application became a serious option?

Charlotte: The salary range. Whether specialist really means specialist, or doing three jobs without a management title.

Oria: Who could give you a more honest picture than the advertisement?

Charlotte: An old colleague works there. I could ask her what a normal week looks like.

Oria: Does that feel manageable?

Charlotte: Yes. Applying feels like betraying everyone. Asking about a week feels like gathering information.

Oria: And at your current job, is there one boundary you'd want to practice regardless of whether you leave?

Charlotte: Asking what gets deprioritized when something urgent comes in. Instead of silently absorbing it.

CLOSING

Charlotte: So you aren't going to tell me to quit.

Oria: No. What would you tell yourself after this conversation?

Charlotte: That looking isn't resigning. And I need to find out whether I want a different employer or a different kind of work.

PROCESS REVIEW

Surface request: a favorable time to change jobs.
Underlying need: permission to choose meaningful work without dismissing loyalty or financial obligations.
Key interaction: Charlotte corrects a simplistic account of her boss; Oria revises her understanding.
Astrological role: structure versus expansion gives Charlotte language for a conflict she then defines herself.
Ending: information gathering and one workplace boundary, rather than an externally imposed resignation decision.
