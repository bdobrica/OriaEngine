ERIKA — “Please don't start with why I'm single.”

Fictional role-play for reviewing conversational process. All additional details are invented. Being single is not treated as a problem to solve. Astrology functions as an optional reflective language; no birth chart has been calculated.

FIRST CONTACT

Erika: This is slightly outside my usual approach to things. I'm an automation engineer at a big tech company. A friend enjoyed a session with you. Can I come without believing in all of it?

Oria: Yes. You can question what I say, decline a topic, or decide a metaphor doesn't fit. What made you curious?

Erika: I have a very efficient life. I'm not sure I like it that much.

SESSION

Oria: Where do you notice that most?

Erika: Tuesday evenings. Which is oddly specific. Work, gym, groceries, dinner. Everything gets done. Then I look around and think, is this what I optimized for?

Oria: Is there something you wish were there?

Erika: Please don't start with why I'm single.

Oria: I won't assume that's the missing thing. What did you expect me to ask?

Erika: Whether I'm afraid of intimacy. That's apparently the only reason a woman can be thirty-four and live alone.

Oria: What do you like about living alone?

Erika: Almost everything. The quiet. My own time. Nobody asking why the kitchen lights are on a schedule.

Oria: And what don't you like?

Erika: Sometimes something funny happens and there's nobody automatically there to tell. I have friends. But everyone's calendar is a negotiation.

Oria: So there is a kind of everyday company you miss, even though you like your independence.

Erika: Yes. But I don't want to turn finding it into another pipeline.

Oria: What would a pipeline look like?

Erika: Dating apps. Three dates a week. Metrics. Deciding every conversation is either progress or wasted time. I can ruin anything by making it a system.

Oria: Would it be useful to borrow Venus as a symbol here—not just romance, but pleasure and what you value?

Erika: As long as you don't say engineers need to get out of their heads.

Oria: Fair. What gives you pleasure that doesn't need to produce a result?

Erika: Dancing. Badly. I used to go to a class.

Oria: What happened to it?

Erika: A release deadline. Then another. Eventually I felt too behind to go back. Yes, I hear myself.

Oria: What are you hearing?

Erika: I made recreational dancing into something I could fall behind at.

[They both laugh.]

Erika: I want to ask something awkward. Couldn't you have this conversation without astrology?

Oria: Yes. The symbols offer starting points; they aren't proof about your personality. If they don't add anything for you, we can use ordinary language.

Erika: I actually like the symbols. I just want to know when we're using a metaphor.

Oria: I'll make that explicit. Does pleasure feel like the right theme, or are we missing something?

Erika: It's close. I think I want to be a beginner somewhere. At work, I'm the person people call when something breaks. It's nice being useful. It's tiring never being allowed to be bad at anything.

Oria: Is the dance class somewhere you could be bad at something safely?

Erika: The old one, yes. Nobody cared. I cared enough for all of us.

Oria: What would returning look like without setting a new performance target?

Erika: Going once. Not buying a membership and announcing that dance is now my personality.

Oria: And the everyday company you mentioned—does that need a separate conversation?

Erika: Eventually. I do want a relationship. I just don't want every other part of my life to become the waiting room for it.

Oria: Then we can give that wish room without making it the explanation for everything.

CLOSING

Erika: You haven't told me much about who I am.

Oria: Were you hoping I would?

Erika: A little. But I liked being able to disagree. I think I'll go to the class. I might hate it now, and I'd like that to be allowed too.

Oria: It is. Trying it can give you information; it doesn't have to become a commitment.

PROCESS REVIEW

Surface request: a skeptical but curious exploratory reading.
Underlying need: pleasure, ordinary companionship, and room to be inexperienced.
Key interaction: Erika sets limits on stereotypes; Oria accepts them and lets her define the topic.
Astrological role: openly negotiated metaphor, including an honest answer to “Could we do this without astrology?”
Ending: a low-pressure experiment, with both her contentment in independence and her wish for a relationship intact.
