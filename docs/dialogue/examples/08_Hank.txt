HANK — “Who am I when nobody needs me at eight?”

Fictional role-play for reviewing conversational process. Astrology is used as an optional symbolic language, not a forecast. All biographical details are invented; no birth chart has been calculated.

BACKGROUND

Hank is 63 and recently retired after thirty-eight years as a railway maintenance supervisor. He is married, has two adult sons, and has always been the person people call when something breaks. He looked forward to retirement with his wife, Ellen, but she has a busy life of her own. He contacts Oria after hearing her on a local radio program discussing transitions. He tells nobody about the appointment.

FIRST CONTACT

Hank: Do you see people who don't know what to ask? I'm not particularly into astrology.

Oria: Yes. What made you consider coming?

Hank: You said on the radio that finishing something can feel different from being finished. That stayed with me.

Oria: We could start there.

SESSION

Hank: I've been retired eleven weeks. Everyone says I must be enjoying it. I say yes because the alternative takes too long.

Oria: What would the longer answer be?

Hank: I get up at six. Make coffee. Wait for the day to start. It doesn't seem to.

Oria: What used to make it feel started?

Hank: Someone needing a decision. A crew waiting. Knowing where to put myself.

Oria: Do you miss the work, or being needed?

Hank: Both. People keep trying to make it one or the other. Some of the work was interesting, you know.

Oria: You're right. I narrowed it too quickly. What do you miss doing?

Hank: Finding a problem before it became an accident. Teaching the young lads to notice things. Having people around who understood why a tiny detail mattered.

Oria: Where do you get that kind of conversation now?

Hank: Mostly I don't. I rang my replacement three times the first week. Then I heard myself.

Oria: What did you hear?

Hank: A man asking whether they were managing without him, hoping the answer was no.

[Hank rubs his hands together and looks toward the window.]

Oria: That sounds difficult to admit.

Hank: He is managing. He's good. I trained him.

Oria: How does that feel?

Hank: Proud. Unnecessary. Both, since we're allowing both.

Oria: We are.

Hank: Does astrology have a name for this bit?

Oria: We could use Saturn as a symbol for responsibility and the structures that organize a life. The question would be which structures you want to keep and which you can set down. That isn't a verdict about your retirement.

Hank: I'd keep being useful. I'd set down being on call. Though lately I'm practically begging people to call.

Oria: Who would you like to spend time with even if they didn't need something repaired?

Hank: My wife. I thought we'd do more together. She says I can't turn her into my new department.

Oria: What had you imagined?

Hank: Breakfast out. Trips. She's got swimming, friends, her own routine. I keep asking what we're doing today. Apparently that's irritating.

Oria: Have you told her what those questions mean to you?

Hank: That I don't know what to do with myself? No. I ask why she's always out.

Oria: How might the conversation change if you said the first thing?

Hank: She might understand. She might also worry. I don't want her cancelling things to supervise me.

Oria: What could you ask for that would feel like company rather than supervision?

Hank: One morning a week that's ours. Planned. So I don't keep hanging around hoping.

Oria: And outside the marriage?

Hank: There's an old colleague I liked. We used to fish. I haven't called because I don't have anything to report.

Oria: Did you need news to enjoy fishing together?

Hank: No. Half the point was not talking.

CLOSING

Oria: What would you like to take away today?

Hank: I don't need a new grand purpose by Friday. But I do need people, and I should probably ask for them without inventing a job first.

Oria: Does that feel like your conclusion?

Hank: Yes. Don't put it on a motivational poster, though.

PROCESS REVIEW

Surface request: help understanding an uncomfortable retirement.
Underlying need: belonging and continuity without making usefulness the price of company.
Key interaction: Hank rejects a false choice between missing the work and missing being needed; Oria corrects course.
Astrological role: responsibility and structure provide a vocabulary for transition, not an explanation imposed on him.
Ending: Hank identifies two invitations he could make. His new identity remains a work in progress.
