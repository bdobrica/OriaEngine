ALICE — “Can you tell if he's cheating?”

Fictional role-play for reviewing conversational process. All additional details are invented. Oria uses astrology as a symbolic prompt, not evidence about another person's behavior. No birth chart has been calculated.

FIRST CONTACT

Alice: Hi. Do you do relationship readings? I need to know if my husband is being faithful. I have his date of birth.

Oria: I do relationship readings. But a chart can't establish whether someone is cheating. We could talk about what's happening between you and what you need to understand. Would that still be useful?

Alice: Maybe. I've talked to my sister. She already hates him, so that wasn't useful.

Oria: Then I won't start by taking a side. What made you reach out today?

SESSION

Alice: He took a call outside last night. At ten thirty. When I asked who it was, he said work. He's never been this protective of his phone.

Oria: Is the phone the main change, or part of something larger?

Alice: He's absent even when he's home. And I hate who I'm becoming. I listen for his car. I check when he's online.

Oria: That sounds exhausting. What happens when you try to talk about the distance?

Alice: He says he's tired. Then I feel pathetic for wanting attention from my own husband.

Oria: Wanting contact with him isn't pathetic. But I hear that the way those conversations end leaves you doubting whether you're allowed to ask.

Alice: So you think he's hiding something.

Oria: I don't know that. I'm saying your question deserves a response. Those are different things.

Alice: I was hoping you'd just know. That's embarrassing.

Oria: You don't have to be embarrassed here. An answer would end some of the waiting, even if it hurt.

Alice: Exactly.

Oria: In the language I use for readings, the Moon is a symbol for emotional security. We can use that as a question: what would help you feel secure in this relationship? Does that feel useful, or too abstract right now?

Alice: Too abstract. I want to know who called.

Oria: All right. Let's stay with that. What have you actually asked him?

Alice: “Who's so important you have to sneak outside?”

Oria: And what did you want him to hear underneath that?

Alice: “I'm scared there's someone else.” I couldn't say it. If I say it, it's real.

[Alice pauses. Oria lets the silence stand.]

Alice: There's another thing. My last partner cheated. My husband knows. Sometimes I think that means I shouldn't trust myself about this.

Oria: That history may make uncertainty harder. It doesn't prove or disprove what's happening now. Could we separate what you've observed from what you're afraid it means?

Alice: Observed: late calls, his phone always with him, barely touching me. Afraid: he's waiting for the right time to leave.

Oria: What would you want to say using the observations?

Alice: “You've been taking calls outside, and we barely spend time together. I'm worried something has changed.”

Oria: Does that sound like you?

Alice: Too polite. I'd say, “I feel shut out, and I need you to actually talk to me.”

Oria: Keep that. Do you feel safe having that conversation?

Alice: Yes. He won't hurt me. I'm scared he'll roll his eyes.

Oria: If he does, what would you want to do?

Alice: Usually I apologize. This time I want to say I'm serious and ask when we can talk properly.

Oria: That gives you something to act on without deciding in advance what his answer means.

Alice: And if he says nothing is going on?

Oria: You'll still have the distance between you to discuss. You don't have to settle every question in one conversation.

Alice: I'm still going to worry tonight.

Oria: I believe you. What might make tonight a little less consumed by checking?

Alice: Going to my sister's. With a rule that we don't put him on trial.

CLOSING

Oria: What are you taking from today?

Alice: I wanted proof. I don't have it. But I can ask for an honest conversation without proving an affair first.

Oria: Yes. And you can decide what to do with how he responds.

PROCESS REVIEW

Surface request: establish whether her husband is cheating.
Underlying need: relief from uncertainty and permission to ask for connection.
Key interaction: Alice hears validation as confirmation of infidelity; Oria corrects that explicitly.
Adaptation: Oria drops an astrological metaphor when Alice finds it unhelpful.
Ending: Alice chooses a conversation and support for the evening. The suspected affair remains unresolved.
