MANDY — “I love my daughter. I miss myself.”

Fictional role-play for reviewing conversational process. All biographical details are invented. Astrology is used for reflection, not diagnosis or parenting prescriptions. No birth chart has been calculated.

BACKGROUND

Mandy is 36, the mother of a three-year-old girl, and a part-time museum educator. Before becoming a parent she was involved in amateur theatre and had a lively social life. She and her husband share a home, but she manages most of the family's planning and coordination. She books Oria herself after realizing that every wish she writes down concerns somebody else's needs.

FIRST CONTACT

Mandy: I want a reading about me. That sounds obvious, but I mean not about my daughter's future or whether I'm a good mother.

Oria: What would you like there to be room for?

Mandy: That's the problem. I can tell you what everyone in my house needs. I don't know my own answer.

SESSION

Oria: What was happening when you decided to book?

Mandy: Someone asked what I wanted for my birthday. I said a better vacuum cleaner. I do want one, but then I got irrationally upset.

Oria: What felt upsetting about the answer?

Mandy: It was like being asked who I was and replying with a household appliance.

Oria: Who would you like them to remember?

Mandy: I used to be funny. I used to go onstage. Now my best performance is making broccoli seem exciting.

Oria: Do you miss performing?

Mandy: Yes. And being expected somewhere because I might be interesting, not because I've packed the snacks.

Oria: What happens when you imagine going back?

Mandy: Guilt. Then logistics. Then exhaustion at the thought of arranging the logistics. So I buy a book about creativity and don't read it.

Oria: Who would need to be involved in making an evening possible?

Mandy: My husband. He's not useless. I need to say that. He does bedtime and cooking. But I still know when the nursery costume day is and which shoes don't fit.

Oria: How do the two of you talk about that difference?

Mandy: He says, “Just tell me what you need.” I know he means it kindly. But I don't want to be the manager of my own break.

Oria: What would shared responsibility look like from your point of view?

Mandy: Him knowing what needs doing without a list from me. Some things being his to remember.

Oria: Is that part of the conversation you want to have, as well as asking for time out?

Mandy: Yes. But I thought we'd talk about my chart, and suddenly I'm negotiating household labor again. That's exactly what I wanted an hour away from.

Oria: Thank you for stopping me. I followed the practical obstacle too far. Let's come back to you. Would you like a symbolic prompt about enjoyment, without turning it into an action plan yet?

Mandy: Please.

Oria: We can use the Sun as an image for expression and feeling present in your own life. When did you last feel absorbed in something simply because you liked it?

Mandy: I read a scene aloud for a friend's audition tape. Ten minutes on the phone. I did the other character in a ridiculous voice. We couldn't stop laughing.

Oria: What did you enjoy about those ten minutes?

Mandy: I wasn't monitoring myself. I wasn't useful. Well, technically I was useful, but that wasn't why I enjoyed it.

Oria: What would you like to say about that version of you?

Mandy: She's not gone. She's just been given a very small room.

[Mandy sits quietly for a moment.]

Mandy: I don't want to sound like I regret my daughter.

Oria: Is that something you feel, or something you fear I'll conclude?

Mandy: What you'll conclude. I love her. I also want something that doesn't have to benefit her to be worthwhile.

Oria: What might that be, if nobody needed you to justify it?

Mandy: A theatre workshop. Not because it would make me a refreshed mother. Because I'd like to act.

CLOSING

Oria: Do you want to leave that as a wish today, or think about a next step?

Mandy: A wish, for five more minutes. Then I'll look up a workshop. But first I'd like to tell you about the worst play I was ever in.

Oria: I'd like to hear it.

PROCESS REVIEW

Surface request: reconnect with an identity beyond motherhood.
Underlying need: enjoyment and attention that do not require a caregiving justification.
Key interaction: Mandy points out that the session is reproducing her usual burden of planning; Oria changes direction.
Astrological role: a symbol of expression opens a specific memory rather than assigning a fixed maternal identity.
Ending: the conversation makes room for pleasure before planning. Mandy retains control of its pace.
