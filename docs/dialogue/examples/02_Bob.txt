BOB — “I'm tired of being a project.”

Fictional role-play for reviewing conversational process. All additional details are invented. Oria is an astrologer, not a mental-health clinician; the exchange does not portray astrology as a treatment for depression. No birth chart has been calculated.

FIRST CONTACT

Bob: Do you do readings for people who feel stuck? I keep letting everyone down. I wondered if there's a reason I'm like this.

Oria: We can explore what “stuck” feels like. I wouldn't use a reading to tell you there's something fundamentally wrong with you. What would you hope to get from meeting?

Bob: An explanation, I guess. Something other than “try harder.”

SESSION

Oria: We don't have to start with your whole history. What has today been like?

Bob: I got dressed for this. That's my achievement. Then I sat in the car for twenty minutes because I didn't want to come in.

Oria: What made coming in difficult?

Bob: Having to explain why a man with a job and a flat can't answer a text message.

Oria: You expected to have to justify how bad you feel.

Bob: Usually I do. People say I have plenty to be grateful for. I know that. It makes it worse.

Oria: You don't need to make a case for feeling this way with me.

Bob: My doctor says it's depression. I'm seeing someone. Before you tell me to do that.

Oria: Thank you for telling me. I won't turn this into another assessment. What were you hoping a reading could offer that those conversations haven't?

Bob: I used to feel like my life had a direction. Now every week is a list of things I'm supposed to do to become functional again.

Oria: And you've had enough of being a project.

Bob: Yes. Exactly that.

Oria: Sometimes I use Saturn as a symbol for responsibility and limits. Would you like to explore that theme, or would it feel like another set of obligations?

Bob: Depends. Are you going to tell me this is a lesson the universe sent?

Oria: No. I don't know a cosmic reason for your suffering. We could use the symbol to look at whose expectations you're carrying. We can also leave it aside.

Bob: My father's, probably. He worked constantly. I take one afternoon off and feel like a fraud.

Oria: What does the accusing voice actually say?

Bob: “Other people manage.”

Oria: And what would you want someone to understand about what managing costs you right now?

Bob: That I do go to work. I do pay the bills. By the time I get home, there's nothing left. Then my friends think I don't care.

Oria: Have any of them said that directly?

Bob: One said, “You could at least reply.” Which is fair.

Oria: It hurt, and you can also understand why they felt hurt.

Bob: I don't want you to tell me I'm secretly amazing. You don't know me.

Oria: You're right. I don't know you well enough for that. I can listen without making you prove your worth to earn the conversation.

[Bob looks down. There is a long pause.]

Bob: That's harder to take in than it should be.

Oria: We can let it sit. There doesn't have to be a response.

Bob: I thought this would involve more talking about planets.

Oria: It can, if that's what you'd like. I followed what you brought in, but I should check whether this is still the conversation you wanted.

Bob: It is. I just don't want homework at the end.

Oria: Then no homework. Is there anything you want to leave with?

Bob: Maybe a way to tell my friend I'm not ignoring him. One sentence. Not a whole confession.

Oria: Want to try it in your own words?

Bob: “I'm having a rough time and replies are hard, but I do want to hear from you.”

Oria: Is that enough?

Bob: I'd add, “Can we just watch something this weekend? I don't want a big talk.”

CLOSING

Oria: How was this compared with what you expected?

Bob: Less impressive. More useful. I still feel bad, though.

Oria: You don't have to leave feeling better for this to have been worth your time.

Bob: Good. Because I was about to pretend.

PROCESS REVIEW

Surface request: an explanation for being “not enough.”
Underlying need: contact without performance, judgment, or another improvement plan.
Key interaction: Bob rejects generic praise; Oria acknowledges the limit of what she knows.
Boundary: existing clinical support stays in place. Oria does not assign depression an astrological cause or promise recovery.
Ending: Bob chooses a small social request. His mood is not magically resolved, and he is allowed to say so.
